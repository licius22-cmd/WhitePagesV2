01 — THE BIGGER PICTURE

Sexual health is
part of overall health.

Erectile dysfunction, or ED, means difficulty getting or maintaining an erection firm enough for sexual activity.

Blood vessels, nerves, hormones, and emotional wellbeing all play a role. Changes in erectile function can be associated with conditions such as diabetes or cardiovascular disease, certain medications, stress, or anxiety. There is no single explanation that applies to everyone. [1]

Persistent changes deserve a conversation with a qualified healthcare professional. An evaluation can help identify possible causes and appropriate treatment options. [2]

A USEFUL DISTINCTION

Learning about a condition is different from diagnosing it. A website, symptom list, or supplement label cannot replace an individual medical evaluation.

02 — EVERYDAY FOUNDATIONS

A health-first approach
starts with the basics.

Daily habits can be part of a broader care plan. They are not a promise of a particular result.

01

Make room
for movement.

Regular physical activity supports overall health. A healthcare professional can help identify an appropriate level of activity, especially when other health conditions are involved. [2]

02

Build a balanced

Vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts, and fish can be part of a balanced diet. Research links healthy eating patterns with erectile health, without establishing any single food as a treatment. [3]

Stress, smoking, and alcohol use can matter. A clinician may recommend changes or counseling as part of care. Prescribed medications should not be stopped without professional advice. [2]

03 — SUPPLEMENT LITERACY

Know what a
supplement can—and
cannot—tell you.

Men’s wellness products are often marketed with language about energy, vitality, or performance. Those words alone do not establish effectiveness for a medical condition.

Read the NIH overview ↗
01

A supplement is not an approved ED treatment.

The FDA does not approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness before they are marketed. A supplement should not be presented as a substitute for diagnosis or medical treatment. [4]

02

“Natural” does not establish safety.

Ingredients can interact with medications. NIH cautions that complementary approaches have not been established as safe and effective treatments for ED. [5]

03

Read beyond the front label.

Review the Supplement Facts, amounts per serving, directions, and warnings. Bring the complete label to a clinician or pharmacist to discuss potential interactions and whether the product is appropriate.

04

Be cautious with dramatic promises.

Instant results, guaranteed outcomes, and claims of replacing prescription treatment deserve scrutiny. Some products marketed for sexual enhancement have contained undeclared drug ingredients. [5]

04 — A CONVERSATION WORTH HAVING

Better questions.
More informed decisions.

A primary care professional or urologist can discuss changes in erectile function, review medications, and explain evidence-based options.

COMMON QUESTIONS

A little more clarity.

Is erectile dysfunction simply part of getting older?

ED is not an inevitable or routine part of aging. Changes in erectile function can have several causes, and a healthcare professional can help investigate them. [6]

Can a dietary supplement treat erectile dysfunction?

Dietary supplements should not be relied on as ED treatments. NIH reports that complementary approaches have not been shown to be safe and effective for treating ED. Discuss available medical options with a qualified professional. [5]

When is a medical conversation appropriate?

Recurring or persistent changes are a reason to seek an evaluation. A clinician can review health history, medications, and other factors rather than assuming that one product or habit will address the cause. [2]

Does this guide recommend a specific product?

No. This guide explains health concepts and supplement considerations. It does not evaluate or recommend a particular product, and it does not establish that a supplement treats a medical condition.
